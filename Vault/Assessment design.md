# Assessment Design

## Question categories
Personality, Communication & Repair, Conflict, Money, Lifestyle, Family & Children, Values & Religion, Career, Trust & Boundaries, Emotional Needs, Adaptability & Growth, Future Planning.

## Question types
7-point Likert, multiple choice, scenario ("what would you do"), priority ranking. Mixed on purpose — not one format throughout.

## What gets estimated
- **Big Five** personality traits
- **Attachment style** (anxiety / avoidance, ECR-S-style items)
- **Interaction style** (v6) — an outspoken-vs-reserved, task-vs-people quadrant, derived from Big Five, deliberately **not** branded as DiSC
- **Type Preferences** (v7) — four dimensional sliders re-projected from Big Five, deliberately **not** branded as MBTI (a trademarked instrument)
- **Worldview axes** (v4) — how couples talk about decisions (family authority, tradition, gender roles, economic independence), scored from ordinary scenario answers, never a labeled "values section"

Every trait reports a **confidence percentage** and never claims certainty. Below a minimum number of answered items, the report says "not enough data" instead of guessing.

## Scoring rules worth knowing
- Some questions score by **similarity** (agreeing is good), some by **complementarity**, and some are **deal-breakers** (flagged, not averaged into the score) — see [[Decisions Log]].
- Weights are editorial judgment, not derived from data — stated plainly in the report. See [[Known Limitations]].
- Consistency-pair questions (same trait, asked twice, different wording) catch contradictory answers and lower confidence.

## Report sections
Executive Summary, Compatibility Index, Personality Dynamics, Communication, Conflict, Money, Lifestyle, Family, Marriage, Emotional Needs, Strengths, Challenges, Suggested Conversations, Recommendations, Appendix. Printable.
